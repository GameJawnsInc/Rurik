# An adversarial review of the movement work, 2026-09-04

**What this is.** The owner asked, on 2026-09-04: *"adversarially review our progress on
the movement code and existing plan to see if we're on the right track or if there are
creative or underlooked alternatives we can use to develop an accurate emulator."* This
is the answer. It was produced by a three-stage agent panel over the tree at `d0cc63c`
(2026-09-04 20:30, the merge of MOVECODE-1z-bg): ten readers built labelled maps of
every corner of the record; seven critics (convergence, premise, method, goal alignment,
alternatives, prior art and the retail referent, process economics) judged from those
maps and were required to verify anything load-bearing in the tree; a merge step
deduplicated 96 raised items into 23 findings and 25 alternatives; and 38 of those 48
items were then handed to independent refuters who were told to break them with
citations. A completeness critic ran last. Counts, models and what was left
unverified are in the appendix.

**Its evidence class is the same as [FINDINGS.md](FINDINGS.md), the 2026-08-06 panel
review.** The panel is an instrument, and this run measured how far to trust it: of the
38 verified items, **1 was confirmed as written, 34 were WEAKENED (right in kind, wrong in
degree) and 3 were REFUTED.** Almost every critic overstated, and the refuters' narrowed
claims are what this document reports. Where a critic's number is quoted, the refuter's
re-derivation is the one given; where the two disagree, the tree wins and the
disagreement is recorded in §5. Claims about the repo cite `path:line` at `d0cc63c` and
are checkable. Every number this review rests on has a read-only script under
[`movement-2026-09-04/`](movement-2026-09-04/) beside this file, so the numbers
regenerate instead of rotting; the scripts are unpromoted and carry no tests.

**Read the verdict, then §1, then §3. §4 and §5 are the audit trail.**

## VERDICT

**The mechanism work is right and the policy built on it is aimed at the wrong
variable.** Everything the arc decoded out of the client held up under attack: the two
world copies, the bake, the 100 u history reprieve and its gates, the separation law,
the plane-lock class, the seam-blind clip, the guard's gate-2 halt. What did not hold
up is the account of why retail's copy stays near the body — and that account is what
the shipped default is built on.

Three facts, each re-derived for this review, carry that.

1. **Retail's client reports exactly as ours does, and retail's copy tracks the body
   because it walks the lead.** The record's law says *"Retail's 0.257 s × 288 = 74.0 u.
   Cadence ratio 7.00×"*, at `studies/movecode/FINDINGS.md:8841-8842` and again in the
   server's own comment at `toolkit/authsrv/authsrv.py:5503-5504`. Over all 61 live connections, retail's
   own client fires the same ~512 u distance trigger during cruise: 454 cruise pairs,
   inter-report gap p50 1.20 s / p90 1.785 s, chord p90 513.8 u, 208 of 454 in the
   1.5–2.0 s bin. The 0.257 s is the lag of retail's *server* copy, fitted from NPC
   follows; it is not a report cadence. Retail's copy stays ~74 u behind a moving body
   because retail grants a 766 u point ahead and lets the copy walk it. That is the D1
   lead — and the shipped default turns it off (`authsrv.py:5641`), so the copy is
   granted the point the body already left and parks there until the next 512 u
   report. Measured on the 13 default-configuration tapes of 2026-09-04, the moving
   world-0 separation reads p50 510–515 u on 8 of them: the law, intact, exactly as the
   record's own derivation predicts. The all-sample p50 that the record quotes as
   "237 → 0" is 0–10 u on the same tapes because the stop echo zeroes the parked
   majority, and `w0score.py` prints CONFIRMED on a tape whose keyboard legs run
   500 u behind. No echo, rate or cadence term can reach retail's magnitude; only the
   lead can, and the lead was convicted for a lock whose cause the arc later found in
   its own guard (§1z-be/§1z-bf) and never re-applied to the conviction.

2. **Nothing shipped since 09-03 has an operator witness, and the acceptance bar that
   would take one passes on the known-bad arm.** The one operator session in the
   arc's window (RUN-FEEL, 24 s, 40 clicks, before the router default) refuted the
   then-shipped fix in the owner's words. The 43 captures since 09-03 13:41 are all
   harness launches — 26 keyboard scripts and 17 scripted-click walks — and the two
   newest defaults shipped at 20:17 and 20:30 with "No run", after the last capture
   at 17:41. The record deferred the witness to "the operator's next ordinary
   session, at zero cost" four times (`FINDINGS.md:9431, 9483, 9722`; `PLAN.md:2154`).
   Meanwhile the wire hard bar that REALFIX-A4 names as the first acceptance test
   reads 0 hard steps on all 41 captures joined to a tape while the tapes show 12 real
   single-sample drawn-body teleports — including three 2,021 u seam teleports under
   the default flags and a 498 u jump on the operator's own session. The bar is blind
   by construction: a snap that lands inside more than 1.04 s of report silence can
   fire neither arm (`toolkit/clientscan/movesync.py:176-183`), and silence is ~80%
   of span in both eras. An operator session scored on the wire alone would read a
   false null.

3. **The retail server ran the client's mover, and the arc ported the judge instead.**
   `studies/srvtree/FINDINGS.md:161-198` names `Engine\Agent\` and `Engine\Map\Path\`
   as shared code, "R3's authority". The arc transcribed AgTrack (the reconcile judge)
   into `agtrack_mirror.py` and routes clicks over its own A* — and no document in the
   record proposes, prices or refuses porting the mover or the tier-3 solver, or
   reading the client's own solved path back out of memory. On r7's 214 tapped
   queries, every one of the client's 168 intermediate waypoints lies on an edge of
   *our* trapezoid decode (103 bit-exact corners, 65 mid-edge, 0 interior; control
   0/300), while our router emits 0 of 82 bit-exact corners. The vocabulary is shared
   static geometry; the emission rule is what differs. This is bounded — 28 of 131
   comparable queries diverge past the reprieve band — but it is the one residual on
   the click channel that no policy has touched, and ROUTER.md files it as "cadence
   cosmetics".

**Are we on the right track?** On instruments and decode, yes, and better than either
existing emulator: Rurik alone models the client's snap test, and its keyboard grant
shape is closer to retail's than OpenTyria's or gw-preservation's. On policy, no: the
last two days built four gates, a plane clip, an origin test and a stationary waiver
around a lead that is OFF, shipped them as "the default" on a code path the default
never reaches, and left the default's own separation unmeasured. The "derive, don't
iterate" direction of 08-30 is right; what is under-derived is which retail mechanism
keeps the copy on the body, and the record's own sentence names the wrong one.

**Underlooked alternatives?** Yes, and the best of them are cheap because the arc
already built their instruments. Ranked after verification in §3: re-open the lead
with the guard fix applied (a desk replay first, one run second); a drawn-body warp bar
that can see this warp class; memory-fed position reports as the "perfect server"
error-bar arm; the client's own solver rule transcribed into `route()`; the file's
point-location DAG replayed offline to derive the client's containment tolerance
without a client; and the built-but-never-aimed 0x0023 checksum oracle at parked
instants. Three routes the panel proposed were killed by its own refuters and are
listed so nobody re-proposes them.
